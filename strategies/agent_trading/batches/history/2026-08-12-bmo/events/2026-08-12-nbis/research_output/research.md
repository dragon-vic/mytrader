# Nebius Group Q2 2026 pre-event memo

## Thesis and the true bar

Nebius remains a credible hypergrowth AI-cloud story, but today’s disclosure is principally an execution test. The valuation no longer needs proof that AI demand exists; it needs evidence that contracted power is becoming revenue-producing capacity without margin erosion or dilutive funding.

Published Q2 revenue estimates conflict because of small samples and outliers. The best current center is **$585–595M**, with **$570–620M** credible: FXEmpire’s live eight-analyst figure is $584.2M, while a $535M Zacks aggregate is depressed by a $350M outlier. My expectation is **about $590–600M revenue, $2.7–2.9B quarter-end ARR, and $165–180M adjusted EBITDA**, equivalent to a 28–31% group margin. Adjusted EPS expectations cluster around **-$0.62 to -$0.70**, but EPS is secondary because GAAP results can be dominated by investment revaluations. [Current eight-analyst estimates](https://www.fxempire.com/stocks/nbis/earnings), [Q1 definitions and financials](https://assets.nebius.com/assets/6aba98d1-946c-4891-a420-d2f0aa60da95/Nebius%20SHL_Q1%202026.pdf)

ARR is not backlog: Nebius defines it as the final month’s **AI-cloud revenue multiplied by 12**. The published-model bar is around $2.8B, but the positive price-clearing bar is now closer to **$600–620M revenue and at least $3.0B ARR**, accompanied by evidence that 2026 revenue can exceed $3.4B or ARR will reach the upper half of the $7–9B range.

Management currently guides to **$3.0–3.4B revenue, $7–9B year-end ARR and roughly 40% adjusted EBITDA margin**. Market forecasts already center near **$3.38B**, at the top of management’s range. Merely retaining the wide guidance would therefore be fundamentally acceptable but potentially sell-the-news; a revenue guide above $3.4B or an ARR range with a $9B-plus lower bound would be genuinely incremental. [S&P-derived forecast, updated August 10](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/nbis/forecast/)

## What is priced in

NBIS closed August 11 at $193.23, then rose about **8.5–9% to roughly $210–211 before its own release**, largely following CRWV’s strong report. The Binance perpetual was near $211 at the research snapshot. Before that sympathy move, August 14 options priced roughly a **13–14% total event move** from the prior close; much of the previously implied upside band has consequently already been consumed. [CBOE delayed options data](https://cdn.cboe.com/api/global/delayed_quotes/options/NBIS.json)

The stock is approximately 35% below its June high but still up about 130% in 2026. At the pre-event price, its market capitalization is roughly 17 times the midpoint of 2026 revenue guidance. Analyst disagreement is unusually wide: the average target is about $251, but the range is $120–410; on August 10 Goldman maintained $286 while D.A. Davidson cut to $175. [Analyst dispersion](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/nbis/forecast/)

Short interest is large—**60.2M shares as of July 31**, about 24% of shares outstanding and roughly 29–30% using narrower vendor float estimates—but borrow remained inexpensive and days-to-cover was only 2.4. It can amplify a real upside surprise, but it is not evidence that shorts are mechanically trapped. [Short-interest data](https://www.marketbeat.com/stocks/NASDAQ/NBIS/short-interest/)

## Decisive operating evidence

The critical chain is:

**contracted power → connected power → active MW → GPU utilization and pricing → ARR and cash flow.**

At Q1, Nebius reported $399M revenue, $1.92B ARR, $129.5M group adjusted EBITDA and a 45% AI-cloud adjusted margin. It had over 3.5GW contracted, but targeted only **800MW–1GW connected power** by year-end; Q1 did not update active MW and management described Q3 as the major capacity step. Active MW and the remaining New Jersey/H2 energization schedule are thus more important than another undifferentiated land or power announcement. [Q1 shareholder letter](https://assets.nebius.com/assets/6aba98d1-946c-4891-a420-d2f0aa60da95/Nebius%20SHL_Q1%202026.pdf)

CRWV’s fresh result—approximately 1.5GW active—validates industry demand, pricing and older-GPU durability, but also highlights Nebius’s deployment gap. It is a scale benchmark, not a direct unit-economics comparison.

Several bullish facts are already public:

- The Meta arrangement contains $12B of dedicated capacity beginning in 2027 and up to $15B of additional capacity that Meta must purchase if Nebius cannot sell it elsewhere. The latter is an occupancy and financing backstop, not ordinary $15B cloud backlog. [SEC contract disclosure](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1513845/000110465926027886/tm268879d1_6k.htm)
- July’s $775M secured facility is priced at SOFR plus 250 basis points and, together with contracted customer cash flows, covers the associated deployment’s capex. It validates asset-backed financing but is small beside current **$20–25B FY26 capex guidance**. [July 17 financing disclosure](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1513845/000110465926084452/tm2620683d1_ex99-1.htm)
- The newest Microsoft capacity tranche was delivered and remaining tranches were described as on schedule. A fresh acceleration or delay—not repetition—is incremental.
- The asset-light expansion model could reduce capital needs, but partner MW, economics and SLA allocation remain undisclosed; it deserves option value, not base-case revenue credit.

From the roughly $210 post-CRWV baseline:

- **Ordinary/in-line:** approximately -6% to +3%. A modest beat with unchanged guidance and no capacity evidence can lose the sympathy premium.
- **Genuine upside:** approximately +8% to +17%, requiring clear ARR/capacity acceleration, resilient margins and stronger forward guidance.
- **Weak disclosure:** approximately -12% to -22% on a revenue/ARR shortfall, soft H2 delivery, margin pressure or renewed funding concerns.
- **Exceptional tails:** roughly +20–30% or -25–35% on a material guidance reset, major new de-risked contract, connected-power cut, customer delay or surprise equity funding.

The setup is mildly downside-skewed after the sympathy gap, although the prior peak-to-event de-risking and large short base temper that asymmetry.

## What would settle the thesis

The strongest confirmation would be **ARR at or above $3B, revenue above roughly $600M, quantified active/connected capacity progress, unchanged or accelerated Microsoft deliveries, and guidance supporting more than $3.4B revenue without margin or financing deterioration**.

The strongest falsifier is a cut to the **800MW–1GW connected-power target** or delay in major H2/customer tranches. Weak active MW or ARR despite higher connected capacity would also show that installation, utilization or customer conversion—not demand—is the bottleneck.

Important unresolved issues are the lack of a recent active-MW disclosure, unquantified asset-light economics, customer/prepayment concentration and the funding burden. Finally, July CPI is scheduled for **08:30 ET, midway through the earnings call**; movements after the first 90 minutes must be benchmarked against QQQ and AI-cloud peers before being attributed wholly to Nebius.
