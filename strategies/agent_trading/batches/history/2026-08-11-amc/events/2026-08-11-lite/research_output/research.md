## Independent view and the true bar

This event has high trading value. Lumentum is experiencing genuine AI-optics scarcity, but the market already expects that scarcity to produce extraordinary FY27 growth. The June-quarter print matters less than whether September guidance and management commentary make that later ramp more achievable.

Lumentum last reported $808.4 million revenue, up 90% year over year, with 47.9% non-GAAP gross margin, 32.2% operating margin and $2.37 adjusted EPS. Its June-quarter guidance was $960 million–$1.01 billion revenue, 35%–36% operating margin and $2.85–$3.05 EPS. Components were 66% of revenue and Systems 34%. [Lumentum’s May 5 release](https://investor.lumentum.com/financial-news-releases/news-details/2026/Lumentum-Announces-Third-Quarter-of-Fiscal-Year-2026-Financial-Results/default.aspx)

Current FactSet estimates are approximately **$987.7 million revenue and $2.97 adjusted EPS for June**, and **$1.157 billion/$3.61 for September**. FY27 consensus is already $5.67 billion revenue and $18.58 adjusted EPS. Stale feeds below company guidance were discarded, as was an uncorroborated social-media claim that September revenue could grow more than 35% sequentially. [Current FactSet estimates](https://www.finanzen.net/schaetzungen/lumentum)

My June-quarter range is **$995 million–$1.03 billion revenue, 48%–50% non-GAAP gross margin, 35.5%–36.5% operating margin and $2.98–$3.18 EPS**, centered near $1.015 billion and $3.10. Gross margin is derived—Lumentum does not guide it—and approximately 49% is plausible but not itself incremental.

For September, my credible range is **$1.14 billion–$1.23 billion revenue and $3.50–$3.95 EPS**, centered around $1.18 billion–$1.20 billion. The distinction that should govern the disclosure analysis is:

- A guide near **$1.16 billion/$3.60 and roughly 37.5% operating margin** merely validates consensus.
- A genuinely incremental package is closer to **$1.20 billion or more, $3.75 or more and at least 38% operating margin**, accompanied by credible multi-quarter capacity and order visibility.

That higher bar follows from the FY27 numbers: after an in-line September guide, the remaining three quarters would still need to average roughly $1.51 billion revenue and $4.99 EPS. A routine beat does not de-risk that slope.

## Decisive business drivers

The causal chain has four engines:

1. **Scale-out now:** high-margin 100G/200G EML laser chips for 800G/1.6T modules, plus Lumentum’s own transceivers.
2. **Scale-across/DCI:** pump and narrow-linewidth lasers and wavelength-management components, currently important margin contributors.
3. **OCS:** backlog above $400 million, with most intended for the September and December quarters, subject to supply execution. No reliable evidence identifies the quarterly split.
4. **CPO:** meaningful scale-out revenue is expected beginning in the December quarter, with larger purchase-order conversion in calendar 2027; scale-up CPO is later.

Management reported EML demand exceeding supply by more than 30%, pump-laser undersupply even greater, and similar constraints in transceivers. The investment thesis therefore depends on yields, electrical-component availability and output—not demand alone. The Japan fab was described as fully allocated; major new Greensboro capacity does not arrive until 2028. [May earnings call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/05/06/lumentum-lite-q3-2026-earnings-transcript/) [OFC strategy presentation](https://static.seekingalpha.com/uploads/sa_presentations/590/124590/original.pdf)

Margin upside can come from component mix, selective pricing, utilization, higher-yield 1.6T products and internal CW lasers. Contrary evidence is Lumentum’s own target model: at $1.25 billion quarterly revenue it contemplated only 45%–48% gross margin, reserving 49%–52% for $2 billion. A systems-heavy quarter, poor 1.6T yields or OCS ramp inefficiency could therefore make 49% transient.

Concentration is material: two customers represented 26% and 12% of March-quarter revenue, and one customer was 25% of receivables. Management has described Google as a major customer, although the filing leaves customers unnamed. [March 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1633978/000162828026030777/lite-20260328.htm)

## Likely directional framework

An ordinary result—June near $1.0 billion/$3.00 and September around $1.15 billion–$1.17 billion/$3.55–$3.65, without stronger multi-quarter evidence—could finish roughly **−4% to +4%**. Sell-the-news risk remains meaningful even inside that region.

A convincingly above-bar September guide, sustained margin expansion and explicit confirmation that OCS, 1.6T and capacity additions are converting on schedule would support approximately **+6% to +12%**. Exceptional evidence—September above roughly $1.20 billion/$3.75, larger orders or earlier multi-quarter conversion—could produce **+13% to +20%**, with a short-covering tail near **+25%**.

A September revenue or margin shortfall, transceiver-mix dilution, or supply-constrained OCS conversion would support **−10% to −17%**. A thesis break—major customer pushout, capacity/yield failure, CPO/OCS delay or unexpected dilution—could extend toward **−20% to −28%**. Asymmetry is mildly negative for a merely good report, but approximately balanced for a genuine surprise because positioning can amplify upside.

## Facts that would confirm or falsify the thesis

Most confirming:

- September guidance clearly above the $1.16 billion/$3.60 consensus-validation floor.
- Non-GAAP gross margin around 49% with a defensible mix-and-yield explanation.
- OCS backlog conversion remains on schedule, with quantified September/December visibility.
- 1.6T yields, internal CW content and EML/pump output rise without worsening shortages.
- CPO revenue and qualification dates remain intact or move forward.

Most falsifying:

- September below roughly $1.14 billion or EPS below $3.50 despite a satisfactory June print.
- OCS language shifts from supply-gated to customer-delayed, or the backlog timetable slips.
- Systems growth overwhelms component economics and reverses margin expansion.
- Hyperscaler concentration, allocation or order timing worsens.
- Architecture moves faster toward alternatives where Lumentum’s EML position has less content.

`COHRUSDT-PERP.BINANCE` has the strongest industry read-through because Coherent overlaps across InP lasers, transceivers, OCS, CPO and scale-across products, although Coherent’s own August 12 report will quickly supersede that signal. [Coherent’s latest results](https://www.coherent.com/news/press-releases/third-quarter-fiscal-year-2026-results) `AAOIUSDT-PERP.BINANCE` is less incremental: AAOI’s August 6 disclosure already established strong 800G/1.6T demand and laser bottlenecks, and it lacks meaningful OCS and scale-across exposure. Lumentum-specific share wins could be adverse—not positive—for either peer.
