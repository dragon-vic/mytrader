# CoreWeave Q2 2026 pre-event memo

## Thesis and the real bar

CoreWeave’s August 11 report has clear trading value. The expected complete repricing is comfortably above 5%, and `CRWVUSDT-PERP.BINANCE` provides a direct causal instrument.

This is an execution-and-financing event disguised as a revenue report. AI-compute demand is demonstrably strong; the unresolved question is whether CoreWeave can turn contracted demand, power and $31–35 billion of planned 2026 capex into customer-accepted capacity, revenue and operating margin quickly enough to cover its capital cost.

A high-end Q2 revenue print and unchanged annual guidance are already expected. The genuinely incremental package requires visible capacity activation, backlog quality, a credible Q3 margin inflection and no new funding or deployment problem.

## Resolved expectations

Management guided Q2 revenue to $2.45–2.60 billion, adjusted operating income to $30–90 million, interest expense to $650–730 million and company-defined capex to $7–9 billion. FY guidance is $12–13 billion of revenue, $900 million–$1.1 billion of adjusted operating income, $31–35 billion of capex and $18–19 billion of exit ARR. [CoreWeave’s May outlook](https://s205.q4cdn.com/133937190/files/doc_financials/2026/q1/CoreWeave-1Q26-Outlook-Presentation.pdf)

The visible revenue consensus is approximately $2.55–2.56 billion; BofA expects a 2.4% adjusted operating margin versus roughly 2.8% Street, while Oppenheimer expects revenue near the high end and FY guidance maintained. [Current earnings preview](https://www.kiplinger.com/investing/stocks/17494/next-week-earnings-calendar-stocks), [BofA preview](https://www.benzinga.com/analyst-stock-ratings/analyst-color/26/07/60556444/coreweave-q2-preview-strong-revenue-growth-108-margin-may-disappoint)

My decision-quality Q2 expectation is:

- Revenue of **$2.56–2.61 billion**, centered near $2.59 billion.
- Adjusted EBITDA of **$1.40–1.47 billion**, around a 55%–57% margin.
- Adjusted operating income of **$60–80 million**, or roughly 2.4%–3.1%.
- Interest expense of **$660–700 million** and company-defined capex near **$8–9 billion**.

Published EPS estimates mix GAAP and adjusted definitions and should not determine the interpretation.

Backlog is the largest hidden bar. Q1 revenue backlog was $99.4 billion and SEC RPO was $98.8 billion, with only 36% expected within 24 months. Formal Q2 consensus near $104 billion is stale relative to Cantor’s widely circulated, assumption-driven inference of approximately $125–131 billion from financing documents. The latter is not directly comparable with reported backlog or GAAP RPO. [Q1 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000176962826000222/crwv-20260331.htm), [Cantor estimate](https://www.tipranks.com/news/the-fly/coreweave-set-to-beat-consensus-backlog-estimates-in-q2-says-cantor-fitzgerald-thefly-news)

I resolve the true headline bar at **$120–130 billion**. Around $131 billion validates the whisper rather than exceeding it; above roughly $135 billion is genuinely incremental. Below $115–120 billion would disappoint even while beating the published consensus. RPO, recognition timing, prepayments and customer credit matter more than the headline total.

For Q3, the credible range is **$3.25–3.45 billion of revenue and $200–280 million of adjusted operating income**. A midpoint below approximately $3.3 billion can be tolerated only if it is explicitly a Q4 timing shift and management fully reaffirms FY revenue, adjusted operating income, exit ARR and year-end active power. A FY raise is not required; a higher FY floor, Q3 revenue above $3.45 billion or exit ARR above $19 billion would be incremental. [Oppenheimer’s July 30 preview](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/articles/coreweave-q2-revenue-expected-apos-172343574.html)

## Operating facts that matter

CoreWeave’s flywheel is take-or-pay booking → contract-backed financing → deployment → customer acceptance → revenue and contribution margin. Q1 revenue was $2.078 billion, adjusted EBITDA $1.157 billion and adjusted operating income only $21 million. Management said new deployments incur one to two months of negative contribution before normalizing around mid-20% contribution margins. [Q1 results](https://investors.coreweave.com/news/news-details/2026/CoreWeave-Reports-Strong-First-Quarter-2026-Results/), [earnings-call transcript](https://s205.q4cdn.com/133937190/files/doc_financials/2026/q1/CoreWeave-Inc-CRWV-US-Q1-2026-Earnings-Call-7-May-2026-5_00-PM-ET.pdf)

There is positive upstream evidence: Core Scientific reached 437 billable MW, while Applied Digital reports 100 MW energized at its 400 MW CoreWeave campus. These are landlord milestones—not proof that CoreWeave has installed, tested and handed capacity to customers. [Core Scientific](https://investors.corescientific.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/139/core-scientific-announces-second-quarter-2026-results), [Applied Digital](https://ir.applieddigital.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001144879-26-000036/apld_invxinvestorpresent.htm)

My inference is that roughly **1.3 GW or more of active Q2 power**, with a specific bridge to over 1.7 GW by year-end, would validate execution. Below roughly 1.2 GW—or disclosure limited to leased or energized MW—would materially weaken it.

Demand itself remains supportive: hyperscalers continue reporting rapid AI-cloud growth and constrained capacity. CoreWeave’s early Rubin validation and Dell’s first Rubin-system shipment demonstrate deployment capability, but not an exclusive moat. [Dell](https://www.dell.com/en-us/blog/dell-first-to-ship-systems-built-on-nvidia-vera-rubin-platform-to-coreweave/)

The counterweight is concentrated and expensive execution. Q1’s top two customers supplied 65% of revenue, all GPUs were NVIDIA, and reported March debt was approximately $24.9 billion before substantial subsequent financing. June unsecured notes cost 9.625% in dollars and 8.5% in euros. [Q1 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000176962826000222/crwv-20260331.htm), [June financing](https://investors.coreweave.com/news/news-details/2026/CoreWeave-Announces-Pricing-of-1-25-Billion-of-Senior-Notes-and-2-Billion-of-Senior-Notes/default.aspx)

## Reaction framework and instruments

- **Ordinary/no-action:** all major metrics cluster near expectations, backlog quality is intact and no new timing information emerges. Complete repricing roughly **within ±5%**, with mixed interpretations capable of widening this to about ±7%.

- **Incremental upside:** backlog at or above the high whisper, active capacity firmly on schedule, Q3 above the central bar and no capex increase. Likely complete move **+11% to +21%**; a guide-up plus financing relief could produce a **+25% to +35%** squeeze tail.

- **Disappointment:** backlog below the buy-side bar, Q3 activation delay, adjusted operating income below approximately $60 million, capex above $35 billion or any FY/exit-ARR reduction. Likely **-14% to -24%**, with a **-28% to -38%** tail for a material delay, financing shock or guidance reset.

The main executable contract is **`CRWVUSDT-PERP.BINANCE`**. In an August 11 morning snapshot it traded near $87.90, with roughly four basis points of positive mark/index basis, neutral funding, $3.2 million of open interest and $7.8 million of 24-hour volume. It listed only on May 18, so it has no earnings history. My 58-session matched-close study found cash/perpetual return beta near one and median absolute basis around eight basis points. Binance’s order-book/EWMA pricing and thin overnight depth can nevertheless create an initial 2–5 percentage-point divergence before cash and perpetual prices converge. [Binance contract mechanics](https://www.binance.com/en/academy/articles/how-to-trade-stock-perpetual-contracts-on-binance), [live ticker](https://fapi.binance.com/fapi/v1/ticker/24hr?symbol=CRWVUSDT)

**`NVDAUSDT-PERP.BINANCE`** and **`DELLUSDT-PERP.BINANCE`** are secondary read-throughs. Ordinary CRWV news should move them little; even extreme attributable demand or capex surprises likely imply only about 1%–3% for NVDA and 2%–5% for DELL. Generic CoreWeave financing trouble has an ambiguous supplier read-through, and a simultaneous infrastructure-peer report will contaminate sector attribution.

## Highest-value confirmation and falsification points

The resumed analysis should prioritize:

1. Tested, active and customer-accepted MW—not landlord MW—and the site-level bridge to year-end.
2. Backlog versus RPO, near-term recognition, prepayments, customer identity and credit quality.
3. Q3 revenue and adjusted-operating-income guidance, plus full FY and exit-ARR treatment.
4. Adjusted operating margin paired with capex, interest expense and deployment economics.

The strongest contrary evidence is genuine industry scarcity, positive landlord progress and real Rubin execution. The unresolved risks are the backlog-whisper methodology, exact customer-accepted capacity, concentrated customer and supplier exposure, expensive financing, and the absence of contract-specific earnings history.
