# AST SpaceMobile Q2 2026 pre-event memo

*Research completed August 10, 2026, before the 20:00 UTC disclosure.*

## Independent view and the market’s real bar

ASTS is an execution-and-timeline event, not primarily an EPS event. The clean Q2 revenue hurdle is only about $34–35 million, but the valuation rests on a rapid transition from gateway/government milestones to scaled commercial service in 2027. The true bar is therefore:

- a credible bridge to at least $110–125 million of second-half revenue;
- dated launches and payload counts supporting the roughly 45-satellite objective in early 2027;
- commissioning and measured performance of Block 2 satellites;
- a specific late-2026 beta plan that validates—not necessarily monetizes—the FY27 service ramp.

Repeating already-public facts—successful launches, “production through satellite 42,” generic late-2026 beta language, or the early-2027 target—would be low-information.

The market has substantially consumed the July deterioration. AST’s July 15 filing moved the launch campaign from about 45 satellites during 2026 to early 2027 and announced another convertible financing; shares repriced roughly 17% by the following close. But “no further deterioration” remains the baseline, so another delay would still be incremental. [July 15 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1780312/000149315226033365/form8-k.htm)

The distinction is material because AST disclosed that BlueBird 7’s $155–160 million carrying value would be written off through Q2 operating expense. Insurance covers an unspecified portion, but recognition depends on recovery becoming probable. Thus a weak reported GAAP EPS is largely anticipated and less informative than revenue, recurring expense, capex, and forward milestones. [SEC loss disclosure](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1780312/000119312526216950/R13.htm)

With Q1 revenue of $14.7 million and Q2 near $35 million, AST needs approximately $100 million in H2 merely to reach the bottom of guidance, $125 million for the midpoint, and $150 million for the high end. Current Street expectations imply roughly low-$50 million Q3 revenue and mid-$60 million Q4 revenue. Maintaining the wide $150–200 million range without explaining those milestones would be necessary but insufficient. [Q1 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1780312/000119312526216946/asts-ex99_1.htm)

June 30 cash and restricted cash of $2.723 billion is already public. ## Operational thesis and what would be incremental

AST has 12 numbered BlueBirds in orbit after BlueBird 7’s loss. Nine—BB1–6 and BB8–10—were publicly confirmed unfolded or operating. BB11–13 reached orbit August 5, but their large arrays had not been publicly confirmed unfolded or commissioned by the event date. “Production through 42” means satellites in varying work-in-progress stages, not 29 completed spacecraft. [August 5 launch release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260805761351/en/AST-SpaceMobile-Announces-Successful-Orbital-Launch-of-BlueBirds-11-12-and-13)

Launch availability is the disclosed constraint on the early-2027 constellation target, but commercial readiness has additional gates: final assembly and environmental testing, AST5000 integration, array commissioning, gateways and carrier cores, and country-specific approvals. Management’s prior schedules deserve a discount: an August 2025 plan anticipated at least five launches by Q1 2026, while only BlueBird 6 launched successfully before quarter-end. [August 2025 deployment plan](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1780312/000095017025106610/asts-ex99_2.htm)

Technology risk is lower but not eliminated. Block 1 demonstrated 98.9 Mbps to an ordinary smartphone; Block 2 is expected to approach twice that performance, but comparable measured Block 2 throughput has not been disclosed. The strongest operational confirmation would be BB11–13 commissioning, measured throughput/capacity, explicit AST5000 status, and BB14–16 completed and assigned to a dated launch—not another component-production update. [May business update](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1780312/000119312526216946/asts-ex99_1.htm)

Regulatory and demand validation are genuine positives: AST has U.S. commercial SCS authority and a broad carrier ecosystem. Contrary evidence is that Starlink/T-Mobile already has a commercial, 650-plus-satellite direct-to-cell service supporting selected data apps, while AST remains pre-service. A limited AST beta matters mainly as proof of the FY27 revenue bridge, not as material FY26 service revenue. [FCC authorization](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-391A1.pdf), [T-Mobile service update](https://www.t-mobile.com/news/network/t-satellite-data-ready-app-expansion)

## Directional framework

A routine report—$30–38 million revenue, broad guidance maintained, BB11–13 still commissioning, and no dated launches beyond BB14–16—belongs in the roughly −6% to +6% ordinary region and carries sell-the-news risk.

A constructive reset—credible $110–125 million H2 revenue visibility, commissioned Block 2 performance, multiple dated launch manifests, and a named beta/service timetable—supports approximately +10% to +20%. Short positioning can extend a genuinely uncertainty-removing disclosure into a +20% to +35% squeeze tail.

A negative reset—revenue below the credible range, guidance below $150 million, beta moving into 2027 alongside another constellation delay, commissioning problems, or materially higher capital needs—supports roughly −18% to −30%. A hard technology or commercial failure could exceed −35%.

The fundamental asymmetry is modestly negative because valuation still assumes a very large FY27 ramp, but upside is more convex because the stock has de-risked from its peak and remains heavily shorted.

The highest-value disclosure facts are therefore the H2 revenue bridge, BB11–13 commissioning and measured Block 2 performance, firm BB14–16 and subsequent launch dates/payload counts, and a carrier-specific beta-to-commercial timetable. Definitive J-LEO terms or a material probable insurance recovery would add upside; conditional project value, WIP satellite counts, and generic partnership language would not.
