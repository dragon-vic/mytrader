# Hims & Hers Q2 2026 pre-event memo

*Research completed August 10, 2026, ahead of the 5:00 p.m. ET earnings call.*

## Thesis and the market’s real bar

This event has clear trading value. HIMS has repeatedly repriced by double digits around earnings, and the August 14 options market implies approximately a ±14.4% move. The central issue is not whether reported revenue exceeds management’s old $680–700 million guidance: that guidance explicitly excluded Eucalyptus, which closed June 2. A consolidated figure near $725 million could therefore be entirely ordinary.

My independent Q2 expectation is:

- Consolidated revenue around **$725 million**, with a credible **$710–740 million** range.
- Legacy Hims revenue around **$697 million**.
- Eucalyptus June GAAP revenue around **$20–35 million**. Its advertised $450 million-plus annual run rate was annualized gross fulfilled billings, not GAAP revenue.
- Adjusted EBITDA around **$50 million**, plausibly **$45–55 million**.
- Subscribers around **2.80 million**, with a wide **2.75–2.85 million** range because Eucalyptus definitions and branded-GLP-1 retention are uncertain.
- Adjusted gross margin around **68%**. Q1’s reported 65% included a $28.5 million restructuring charge; the comparable underlying margin was 70%.

Current estimate feeds range from stale, acquisition-excluding revenue near $690 million to fresher estimates of $714–730 million. The most defensible current market bar is therefore **approximately $720–730 million**, not $690 million. EPS estimates should be largely ignored: vendors use irreconcilable adjustment definitions, ranging from losses to positive adjusted EPS. Revenue composition, EBITDA, margins and guidance carry more information. ## What would actually be incremental

A headline FY revenue raise is also insufficient. My consolidated FY expectation is roughly **$3.08 billion revenue and $300 million adjusted EBITDA**, with credible ranges of **$3.00–3.15 billion** and **$285–315 million**. A midpoint near $3.10 billion would be mostly acquisition rebasing; genuinely better information would be an explicit legacy-business outlook above the previous $2.90 billion midpoint or consolidated revenue guidance centered nearer $3.15 billion, while preserving at least approximately $300 million of EBITDA.

The harder hurdle is the implied second-half profit acceleration. With Q1 EBITDA of $44.3 million and Q2 likely near $50 million, a $300 million year requires about **$206 million in H2**, roughly twice the quarterly Q2 run rate. Management attributes that step-up to monthly branded-GLP-1 cohorts stacking, retention, marketing efficiency and G&A leverage. Evidence that Q3 EBITDA remains below roughly the $80–90 million area would undermine the bridge even if Q2 revenue beats. [Q1 earnings-call transcript](https://s27.q4cdn.com/787306631/files/doc_financials/2026/q1/Hims-Hers-First-Quarter-2026-Earnings-Call-Transcript-doc.pdf)

The decisive business question is whether Hims owns a durable patient relationship or is becoming a lower-margin distributor of medicines available through many channels. More than 125,000 Wegovy shipments in the first six weeks demonstrated launch velocity, not unique or retained customers. The required weight-loss membership rises from $39 in month one to $149 thereafter, creating a meaningful renewal hurdle. BofA’s June data suggested oral-Wegovy additions had already flattened, so management’s retention commentary matters more than shipment volume. [Hims pricing](https://www.hims.com/weight-loss/faq), [BofA review](https://www.investing.com/news/analyst-ratings/bofa-raises-hims-and-hers-stock-price-target-to-37-on-growth-93CH-4783362)

Management’s claim that branded and compounded patients generate “roughly comparable” contribution dollars remains unverified. It becomes credible only with a mix-to-margin bridge, stable cash generation and evidence that monthly fulfillment and care costs are not eroding contribution. Cross-selling weight-loss customers into testosterone, menopause, Labs and established categories would be stronger evidence of platform value than GLP-1 volume alone.

Novo access is useful but nonexclusive, while Hims has no formal Lilly affiliation. Peptides remain long-dated optionality: July’s favorable advisory votes were nonbinding and contribute nothing to the reasonable 2026 base case. The July 29 FTC allegations are already known, but disclosure of higher refunds, easier cancellation hurting retention, consent changes impairing conversion, or rising acquisition costs would be genuinely new operating information. [Novo agreement](https://www.novonordisk.com/content/nncorp/global/en/news-and-media/news-and-ir-materials/news-details.html?id=916512), [FDA peptide meeting](https://www.fda.gov/advisory-committees/advisory-committee-calendar/july-23-24-2026-meeting-pharmacy-compounding-advisory-committee-07232026), [FTC complaint](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/07/ftc-states-act-against-hims-hers-deceptive-unlawful-privacy-practices)

## Pricing, positioning and contract transmission

HIMS ended August 7 at $31.59 and traded near $31.09 before the event. It has recovered about 26% from the FTC-related trough and stands above the $29.23 average analyst target, suggesting that an ordinary revenue beat is consumed. It nevertheless remains roughly 16% below early-July levels, leaving room for a genuine earnings-power reset. Approximately 29–30% of float is short, providing substantial upside squeeze fuel while also documenting deep fundamental skepticism. [Price and analyst history](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/hims/forecast/), [positioning](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/hims/statistics/)

The sole defensible executable instrument is **`HIMSUSDT-PERP.BINANCE`**; Novo, Lilly and other telehealth names have weak or sign-ambiguous causal paths. The contract was listed only in June and has no earnings precedent. At the research snapshot, its basis was approximately -8 basis points, funding had remained zero, and 24-hour volume/open interest were only about $0.45 million/$0.68 million—adequate for price evidence but a thin environment for a major after-hours disclosure. ## Repricing framework

Across the last 12 earnings events, the typical absolute first-session move was approximately 6–9%; a recent nine-event sample produced a roughly 12.8% median two-session repricing. Reversals during the call or next cash session have been common, so the first after-hours jump is not the complete move. The current ±14.4% option price is expensive relative to a typical first close but reasonable for the full release/call/next-session process. [Historical earnings reactions](https://fintel.io/seps/us/hims), [Cboe option chain](https://cdn.cboe.com/api/global/delayed_quotes/options/HIMS.json)

- A clean upside reset—strong legacy growth, durable branded retention, EBITDA above the current bar and a credible H2 bridge—could produce a **+12% to +22%** complete repricing. Short covering creates a **+25% to +35%** tail.

- A consolidated $720–735 million result with ordinary margins, little retention disclosure and mostly mechanical guidance would likely finish around **-6% to +7%**. Given the recent rebound, this region is modestly skewed toward a sell-the-news fade; a final move below 5% is the genuinely low-impact outcome.

- Weak legacy revenue, EBITDA below the mid-$40 millions, adjusted gross margin near or below 66%, branded churn, or a materially weaker H2 outlook supports **-15% to -25%**, with an approximately **-30%** thesis-break tail.

Thus the ordinary case is somewhat downside-skewed, while the most extreme magnitude may be on the upside because of the short interest. The thesis is confirmed most strongly by transparent legacy/Eucalyptus reporting, retained branded customers, stable contribution economics and a believable H2 margin bridge. It is falsified by acquisition-masked organic weakness, post-promotion churn, continued margin deterioration or evidence that FTC remediation damages the subscription funnel.
