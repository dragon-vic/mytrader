# Rocket Lab Q2 2026 pre-event memo

## Thesis and the market’s real bar

This event passes the trading-value screen decisively. Rocket Lab’s historical earnings reactions, extreme valuation, unresolved Neutron schedule, and Iridium financing make a complete issuer-specific repricing above 5% highly plausible.

My base expectation is a modest operating beat: approximately **$237 million revenue, 35% GAAP gross margin, 40% non-GAAP gross margin, and a $21–23 million adjusted EBITDA loss**. The observable revenue feeds span roughly **$231.6–236.0 million**, but $236–240 million is the more realistic reaction bar: Q1 finished just above its guidance ceiling, while current Q2 consensus remains inside management’s $225–240 million range. [Rocket Lab’s Q1 release and Q2 guidance](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1819994/000181999426000027/rklb-05072026ex991.htm), [current multi-analyst estimate](https://www.chartmill.com/stock/quote/RKLB/analyst-ratings), [lower Zacks estimate](https://www.zacks.com/stock/news/2962615/rocket-lab-corporation-rklb-stock-dips-while-market-gains-key-facts).

Consequently, a low-feed “beat” around $233 million would not necessarily be bullish. The true bar is a high-end Q2 result accompanied by evidence that growth, margins, Neutron and financing remain credible.

## Expectations and decisive operating facts

Q2 had six launches, flat sequentially, while Space Systems should benefit from defense production and approximately $15 million of initial Mynaric revenue. Management warned that a lower high-margin launch mix and initially low-margin Mynaric work would reduce gross margin from Q1. That supports a roughly **$60 million Launch / $177 million Space Systems** mix and a result near the top, rather than materially above, guidance. [Q1 call transcript](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/rklb/transcripts/572358-q1-2026/), [Q1 segment disclosures](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1819994/000181999426000028/rklb-20260331.htm).

Headline EPS is a definition trap. Company guidance implies roughly **-$0.07 to -$0.08 GAAP EPS before unquantified purchase-accounting, acquisition-related stock compensation and later Iridium transaction costs**. Several -$0.06/-$0.07 feeds exclude non-recurring items. A reported -$0.09 or -$0.10 could therefore coexist with an operating beat; revenue, margins, adjusted EBITDA and guidance deserve more weight than unreconciled EPS.

For Q3, the credible consensus range centers near $236–240 million. With Q1 actual revenue of $200.3 million and my $237 million Q2 estimate, current full-year expectations around $919–936 million require approximately $486–499 million in the second half. My interpretation is:

- A **$238–243 million Q3 midpoint** broadly maintains the existing path.
- **At least $247 million**, with stable or improving margins and EBITDA, is genuinely incremental.
- **$233–235 million or less** creates uncomfortable Q4 back-loading. [TwelveData consensus snapshot](https://twelvedata.com/markets/980755/stock/six/rklb/analysis), [StockAnalysis annual consensus](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/rklb/forecast/).

Backlog quality matters more than another headline total. Q1 backlog was $2.22 billion, but only 36% was expected within twelve months and contracts can include termination rights. The subsequent $266 million launch award contains six optional missions, while the August 4 $397 million Flatellite total also contains undisclosed options; neither post-quarter headline should simply be added to June 30 backlog. Incremental information would be firm value, funding, conversion timing and cost-to-complete—not repetition of already-public awards. [Q1 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1819994/000181999426000028/rklb-20260331.htm), [$266 million government award](https://www.war.gov/News/Contracts/Contract/Article/4551533/contracts-for-july-21-2026/), [Flatellite announcement](https://investors.rocketlabcorp.com/news-releases/news-release-details/rocket-lab-awarded-397-million-contract-build-and-launch).

## Forward issues that can override the quarter

**Neutron is the largest single sensitivity.** The January first-stage tank rupture led to manufacturing and test-plan changes, with a Q4 2026 target subsequently reiterated. A July full-duration vacuum-engine firing was progress, but it did not establish completion of the replacement first-stage qualification, sea-level engine qualification or integrated nine-engine testing. Absence of a public milestone is not proof of delay; it leaves schedule buffer unknowable. [Tank-test update](https://rocketlabcorp.com/updates/rocket-lab-neutron-test-update/), [current Neutron roadmap](https://rocketlabcorp.com/launch/neutron/).

Concrete confirmation would include a qualified replacement tank, closed engine qualification, flight hardware at Wallops and dated integrated static-fire and regulatory milestones. An explicit 2027 target, redesign, engine-production blocker or licensing critical path would outweigh an ordinary Q2 beat. This sensitivity has increased because the newly announced Flatellite execution plan expressly uses Neutron.

**Iridium is the other forward fulcrum.** The approximately $8 billion enterprise-value transaction uses cash, RKLB stock and a $3.6 billion bridge facility and is not part of Q2 consolidated operations. The market needs credible permanent financing, integration economics and regulatory timing. [Transaction filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1819994/000175392626001085/g085783_ex99-1.htm). Likewise, the May $3 billion ATM is authorization—not evidence that shares were issued. Actual Q2 utilization would be incremental; unused capacity is already known. [ATM filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1819994/000181999426000045/rklb-20260520.htm).

## Likely full-event repricing

An ordinary report—approximately $232–240 million revenue, in-range margins, a $238–243 million Q3 midpoint, and vague but unchanged Neutron language—has a likely complete-event region of roughly **-6% to +5%**. The recent run-up gives that outcome a mild sell-the-news bias.

A clearly constructive bundle—high-end revenue, margins and EBITDA, Q3 at least around $247 million, credible backlog conversion and concrete Neutron progress—supports approximately **+8% to +18%**. A genuine guide raise combined with major technical or financing de-risking could reach **+18% to +30%**.

A soft guide, margin/cash deterioration, actual dilution, or credible forward weakening supports **-10% to -22%**. A hard Neutron delay, test failure or materially stressed Iridium financing creates a **-25% to -35%** tail. Even with an operating beat, a meaningful but non-catastrophic Neutron or financing downgrade would probably dominate the call digestion and leave a full move around **-8% to -15%**.

Overall, upside squeeze risk remains real because the stock is far below its peak and short interest is meaningful. The probability distribution is nevertheless modestly downside-skewed: recent awards and mission success are already public, while the valuation still requires unusually strong conversion, financing discipline and schedule execution.
