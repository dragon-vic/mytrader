# Ondas Q2 2026 pre-event memo

*Information state: approximately 09:30 UTC on August 13, ahead of the scheduled results.*

## Thesis and market bar

This is an **acquisition-conversion and forward-credibility event**, not an EPS event. Ondas must show that acquired backlog is becoming revenue without sacrificing margins, and that its extreme second-half ramp rests on funded deliveries rather than tender ceilings or additional M&A.

Published Q2 revenue estimates cluster around **$65.8–69.3 million** ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/quote/ONDS/earnings), [MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/quote/stock/ONDAS-INC-211784974/finances/)). My expectation is **$68–74 million, centered near $71 million**. Full-quarter-equivalent Q1 revenue for the then-owned businesses was roughly $59 million; Q2 adds World View from April 1, Mistral from April 24 and Omnisys from May 21, but Mistral’s major loitering-munition deliveries begin in Q3 and Bird’s Q1 shipments may have been unusually strong. Around **$75 million with 45–49% GAAP gross margin** would therefore be a clean operating beat; a high-$60 million result is merely in line with the effective bar.

EPS is secondary because warrant and acquisition remeasurements can dominate GAAP results. Q1 produced $361 million of GAAP net income despite a $42.7 million operating loss, principally because of a $389.5 million non-cash warrant gain. Revenue, arithmetic gross margin, adjusted EBITDA, cash operating expense and operating cash flow are cleaner measures. My Q2 adjusted-EBITDA expectation is a **$20–30 million loss**, consistent with management’s warning that Q2 would probably be the annual loss peak. [Q1 release](https://ir.ondas.com/press-releases/detail/306/ondas-reports-record-first-quarter-2026-financial-results)

## Decisive operating evidence

The March $457 million “pro forma backlog” comprised approximately $177 million of Ondas orders in hand, $264 million of Mistral contracted backlog and $16 million from World View. It excludes the $982 million ceiling on Mistral’s Army IDIQ, but “contracted” does not establish that every dollar is funded. Ondas also announced more than $150 million of Q2 orders and awards; these cannot safely be added wholesale to backlog because recognition, overlap and funding status were not reconciled. [Mistral reconciliation](https://ir.ondas.com/press-releases/detail/304/ondas-completes-merger-of-u-s-defense-prime-contractor), [Q2 order update](https://ir.ondas.com/press-releases/detail/313/ondas-secures-over-40-million-in-new-orders-for-autonomous)

The disclosure becomes materially better if it:

- Bridges the $457 million balance through Q2 bookings, revenue and cancellations, then separately adds DZYNE and Cyberhawk under consistent definitions.
- Separates legacy/Airobotics/Sentrycs growth from acquired revenue and shows gross margin resilience.
- Establishes funded Q3–Q4 delivery schedules for Mistral, INDO, demining and C-UAS programs.
- Preserves the $525 million pre-Cyberhawk economics rather than absorbing Cyberhawk into an unchanged target.
- Shows product-company profitability or improving cash efficiency beneath elevated central integration spending.

Demand is real—counter-UAS, attritable strike, ISR and autonomous ground systems are funded priorities—but transfer to Ondas is not automatic. DZYNE did not rank among the first Drone Dominance program winners; large demining and INDO headlines contain initial orders smaller than their tender ceilings; and the “system-of-systems” thesis still lacks much evidence of paid, integrated multi-product deployments. [Drone Dominance leaderboard](https://dronedominance.mil/leaderboard.html)

## Likely repricing and falsification

From the elevated pre-disclosure perpetual level, an ordinary high-$60 million quarter with no clean backlog bridge or scope-adjusted guidance improvement has a **sell-the-news-skewed range of roughly -8% to +4%**. Credibly incremental conversion, margin and guidance evidence supports **+8% to +18%**, with a **+25–30% squeeze tail**. Weak revenue, qualified guidance, poor margin or integration evidence supports **-15% to -25%**, with approximately **-30%** possible in a severe disappointment.

The thesis is most strongly confirmed by revenue near or above $75 million, resilient gross margin, clearly funded backlog conversion and an FY outlook that distinguishes Cyberhawk scope from underlying improvement. It is falsified by low-$60 million or worse revenue, an unchanged $525 million target newly presented as including Cyberhawk, opaque or ceiling-heavy backlog, delivery slippage, deteriorating product economics, or another large issuance obscuring operating progress.

The largest unresolved uncertainty is definition quality: current consensus EPS, fully diluted shares, “orders,” tender awards and acquired backlog are not consistently comparable. Direction should therefore be anchored to the revenue/margin/cash bridge and the funded second-half delivery schedule, not the headline EPS or nominal backlog total.
