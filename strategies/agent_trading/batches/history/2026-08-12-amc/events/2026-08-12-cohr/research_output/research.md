# Coherent FY2026 Q4 pre-event memo — 12 August 2026

## Thesis and the market’s true bar

Coherent has high event-trading value. The direct executable instrument is `COHRUSDT-PERP.BINANCE`; `LITEUSDT-PERP.BINANCE` and `AAOIUSDT-PERP.BINANCE` are useful peer evidence, but only COHR has the direct issuer-specific causal path. Results are due after the US close and the call begins at 4:30 p.m. ET, according to the [company’s event announcement](https://www.coherent.com/news/press-releases/fy2026-fourth-quarter-fy2026-conference-call-announced).

The central issue is no longer whether AI optical demand is strong. Lumentum established that again on 11 August with revenue and guidance materially above expectations; COHR consequently rose about 7.4% to roughly $352 by 11:40 a.m. ET on 12 August. The disclosure must now show that Coherent is converting that demand into company-specific revenue, capacity, market share and margin.

My view is therefore two-sided but downside-skewed: a merely respectable quarter can sell off because the generic demand validation has already been consumed. Durable upside requires both a strong quarter and forward evidence that Coherent is matching peers in 1.6T, optical-circuit switching, indium-phosphide capacity and margins.

## Decisive operating evidence

Data Center & Communications represented about 75% of Q3 revenue: $1.362 billion, up 41% year over year, versus $444 million from Industrial. A reasonable Q4 model requires roughly $1.54–$1.56 billion from D&C and $450–$470 million from Industrial. The [Q3 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/820318/000082031826000013/iivi-20260331.htm) provides the segment definitions and reported figures.

The most important forward facts are:

- Whether management reaffirms that InP capacity will double during the September quarter, and provides evidence on six-inch wafer yields and output—not merely the previously announced capacity plan.
- Actual 800G and 1.6T shipment growth, product mix and margins. The faster-growing 1.6T products are expected to carry better economics.
- Whether the claimed OCS bottleneck has translated into shipments and whether scale-out co-packaged-optics revenue remains on schedule for the second half of calendar 2026.
- Gross-margin conversion. Revenue growth accompanied by margin around 40% or continued operating-cost pressure would compare poorly with peer execution.
- Capex and free-cash-flow consequences of the capacity build.

Management’s capacity, product and CPO milestones are discussed in the [May earnings call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/05/06/coherent-cohr-q3-2026-earnings-transcript/). NVIDIA’s multibillion-dollar purchase commitment and $2 billion equity investment validate the demand backdrop, but the arrangement is non-exclusive and already known; it is not fresh upside by itself ([official partnership release](https://www.coherent.com/news/press-releases/nvidia-and-coherent-announce-strategic-partnership)).

Peer evidence cuts both ways. Lumentum’s 11 August print—$1.01 billion of revenue, 50%-plus gross margin and a sharply higher next-quarter guide—confirms exceptional optical demand but raises the risk that COHR is converting it less effectively ([Lumentum results](https://investor.lumentum.com/financial-news-releases/news-details/2026/Lumentum-Announces-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Fiscal-Year-2026-Results/default.aspx)). Applied Optoelectronics has also reported rapidly increasing 800G volumes and a large initial 1.6T order ([AAOI results](https://investors.ao-inc.com/news-releases/news-release-details/applied-optoelectronics-reports-second-quarter-2026-results), [1.6T order](https://investors.ao-inc.com/news-releases/news-release-details/aoi-receives-first-volume-order-16t-data-center-transceivers)).

## Directional framework and falsification points

My calibrated ordinary complete-event range is approximately **-12% to +10%**, with a broader realistic range of **-18% to +15%** and tails near **-22%/+20%**. A directional move above 5% is more probable than a genuinely quiet outcome.

- Sustainable upside likely requires something close to the top of Q4 guidance, a materially stronger Q1 outlook than published consensus, margin expansion, and concrete proof of six-inch, 1.6T or OCS conversion. That supports roughly high-single- to low-double-digit upside, with a larger tail if FY2027 estimates reset toward $10 billion/$9.
- A numerically adequate quarter around $2.0 billion and $1.63, followed by guidance near published Q1 consensus, would likely feel stale after the peer-led rally and can produce mid- to high-single-digit downside.
- Guidance below formal consensus, evidence of relative share loss, or delays in InP capacity, OCS or CPO would support a roughly 12%–18% decline.
- A sub-5% complete move probably requires offsetting evidence: a respectable top-end quarter and near-elevated forward guide, but insufficient information to reset FY2027 estimates.

The thesis would be falsified positively by clear company-specific share gains, gross margin above the current trajectory and demonstrable early capacity conversion. It would be falsified negatively by strong industry demand accompanied by weak COHR D&C growth or margin—evidence that Lumentum and other suppliers are capturing the economics.

The largest unresolved uncertainties are product-level revenue opacity, unnamed customer concentration, whether six-inch capacity is producing qualified saleable volume, the staggered CPO timetable, Chinese InP-substrate exposure, industrial cyclicality and the free-cash-flow cost of expansion.
