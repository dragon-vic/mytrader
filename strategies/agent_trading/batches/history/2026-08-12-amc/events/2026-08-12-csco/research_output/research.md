# Cisco FY26 Q4 pre-event memo — 12 August 2026

## Thesis and true bar

Cisco’s quarter is unlikely to determine the reaction; FY27 guidance and AI-order conversion will. Strong AI networking demand is already the market premise. Consensus calls for Q4 revenue of **$16.82–16.83B** and adjusted EPS of **$1.17**, essentially Cisco’s own $16.7–16.9B/$1.16–1.18 guidance midpoint. UBS expects roughly 26% Networking growth, approximately 29% product-order growth, and gross margin near 66%. [Kiplinger/Refinitiv, 7 August](https://www.kiplinger.com/investing/stocks/17494/next-week-earnings-calendar-stocks)

The harder bar is FY27: current consensus is about **$68.8B revenue and $4.79–4.80 adjusted EPS**. [S&P Global estimates, updated 10 August](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/csco/forecast/), [Trefis](https://www.trefis.com/data/companies/CSCO). That is already above the simple management framework:

> ($62.9B FY26 revenue − ~$4B FY26 AI revenue) × 1.04–1.06 + $6B FY27 AI revenue = **$67.3–68.4B**

Reaching $68.8B requires roughly **$6.4B AI revenue even with 6% base-business growth**, or base growth above 6% if AI remains at $6B. Consequently, merely reporting a clean Q4 and reiterating “at least $6B” of FY27 AI revenue is not clearly positive.

My independent ranges are:

- Q4: **$16.85–16.95B revenue, $1.17–1.19 adjusted EPS, 65.8–66.2% adjusted gross margin**
- Indicative Q1 FY27: **$16.75–17.00B, $1.14–1.18**
- FY27: **$67.7–69.1B, $4.60–4.92**, centered near $68.4B/$4.76

The Q1 estimate is lower-confidence because reliable current aggregates are sparse. A superficial beat inside these ranges is financially ordinary; genuinely incremental upside likely requires FY27 guidance at or above $68.8B/$4.80, or component guidance clearly supporting it.

## Decisive operating evidence

Cisco ended Q3 with **$5.3B of FY26 hyperscaler AI orders** and raised its full-year expectation to approximately **$9B**. It therefore needs roughly **$3.7B in Q4**, almost twice Q3’s $1.9B. I found no credible published $10B whisper: $9B is the disclosed anchor, not proven upside. Materially exceeding it would be incremental. [Cisco Q3 results](https://investor.cisco.com/news/news-details/2026/CISCO-REPORTS-THIRD-QUARTER-EARNINGS/default.aspx)

The business evidence is real but concentrated:

- Networking represented about 56% of Q3 revenue and grew 25%; Security was flat and Services declined 1%.
- Product orders rose 35%, or 19% excluding hyperscalers, but management said higher prices contributed roughly 4–5 percentage points. Underlying ex-hyperscaler demand was therefore closer to the mid-teens.
- Acacia recorded more than $1B of Q3 orders. New P200 scale-across wins should begin contributing in FY27, making production timing more important than additional product announcements.
- Campus orders rose more than 25%, but Security’s legacy decline and Splunk’s cloud transition remain drags. [Q3 call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/05/14/cisco-csco-q3-2026-earnings-transcript/)

Industry demand is not the weak link: hyperscaler spending remains exceptionally strong, while Arista, Broadcom and other networking suppliers are also growing rapidly. A Cisco AI-order miss without a named customer delay would therefore lean toward design-win timing, competition or execution rather than an industry-wide collapse. IDC nevertheless shows the competitive danger: NVIDIA became the largest datacenter Ethernet-switching vendor in calendar Q1, while Cisco retained broader market leadership. [IDC, July 2026](https://www.idc.com/resource-center/blog/nvidia-becomes-1-in-datacenter-ethernet-switching-as-1q26-market-surges-39-8-to-15-4-billion/)

Margins are the principal counterweight. Q3 product gross margin fell 250 basis points as higher Networking mix and memory costs outweighed productivity. Inventory increased 49% from fiscal year-end and purchase commitments 111%, primarily to secure Silicon One, hyperscaler-product and memory supply. That supports shipment readiness but creates downside if orders slip; customer concentration, cancellation rights and conversion schedules remain undisclosed. [Cisco Q3 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/858877/000085887726000078/csco-20260425.htm)

## Reaction framework

A **structural upside reset**—FY26 AI orders around or above $9.5–10B, FY27 AI revenue nearer $7B, FY27 revenue above roughly $69B, EPS around $4.90+, and gross margin holding near 66%—supports a complete **+8% to +15%** repricing, with a May-like **+15% to +20%** tail.

An **ordinary beat**—Q4 around $16.9B/$1.18, approximately $9–9.4B AI orders, FY27 near $68.3–68.8B/$4.70–4.80 and 66% margin—does not clear the consumed narrative. A **−3% to −8%** sell-the-news response is plausible. Mixed guidance whose detailed arithmetic still supports consensus defines the roughly **±3% ordinary/no-action region**.

A **fundamental disappointment**—FY26 AI orders below roughly $8.5B, FY27 revenue below $67.5–68B, EPS below $4.70, gross margin below approximately 65.5%, or delayed/lost P200 wins—supports **−8% to −15%**, with a larger downside tail if rising supply commitments coincide with material order weakness.

Overall, the realistic core complete-event range is approximately **−10% to +12%**, with tails toward **−15% and +20%**.

The only clean executable instrument is **`CSCOUSDT-PERP.BINANCE`**. `CIENUSDT-PERP.BINANCE` provides optical-demand context but can benefit from Cisco share loss, so it is not a direct proxy. `COHRUSDT-PERP.BINANCE` reports its own earnings at the same 16:30 ET time and cannot cleanly isolate Cisco information. [Coherent event notice](https://www.coherent.com/news/press-releases/fy2026-fourth-quarter-fy2026-conference-call-announced)

The disclosure facts with the greatest confirming or falsifying power are:

- FY26 hyperscaler AI orders versus the approximately $9B target, plus customer/timing explanation;
- FY27 AI revenue versus the $6.4–7B needed to clear current total-revenue expectations;
- formal FY27 revenue, EPS, gross-margin and operating-margin assumptions;
- P200 scale-across production timing and Acacia/system contribution;
- ex-hyperscaler order growth after separating price from units;
- campus durability, Security’s legacy drag and Splunk cloud-transition progress;
- whether elevated inventory and supply commitments are matched by firm customer demand.

The principal unresolved uncertainty is guidance conservatism: a headline below consensus may be less bearish if detailed components plainly support later upside. Conversely, “approximately $9B” orders are not equivalent to disclosed, noncancelable backlog, so an apparently strong order number still needs credible conversion evidence.
